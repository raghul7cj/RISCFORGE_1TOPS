1Executive summary

The SoC routes UART, SPI, I2C and GPIO into the core's fast local interrupts (mip[19:16]) and Timer0's compare match into MTIP. A fixed if/else chain in top.v arbitrates them, ordered by cause number. There is no PLIC and no CLINT: ext_irq_i and sw_irq_i are tied to 0.

The review found one defect that will drop interrupts on silicon, one violation of the privileged spec, and one priority-policy problem. Peripheral starvation, the original concern, is real but secondary at MCU event rates. It needs a bounded fairness rule, not a heavyweight controller.

Must fix before tape-out

Three of the five live sources are single-cycle pulses: SPI events_o[0], Timer0 compare and GPIO in its reset mode. They feed a latch-free mip, so a pulse is lost whenever EX cannot accept it in that exact cycle. That happens inside any other ISR (mstatus.MIE = 0), during a divide stall, on a pipeline bubble, or behind an exception.

2Current implementation

The path from source to handler:

  1. csr_regfile.v:286 builds mip_w combinationally from the input pins: {fast_intr_i, 4'b0, ext_intr_i, 3'b0, timer_intr_i, 3'b0, msip, 3'b0}. Only MSIP is a register.
  2. top.v:215 computes active_irqs = mip & mie.
  3. top.v:232–256 selects one cause with a fixed chain, 31 down to 16, then 11, 7, 3.
  4. top.v:219 gates it: irq_pending = mstatus.MIE & |active & (cause > mintprio[4:0]).
  5. execute_cycle.v:440 accepts it only when valid_E & ~new_exc & ~exc_valid_E & ~stallMUL. The EX instruction becomes an interrupt packet with mepc = PCE and the front end is flushed.
  6. wb_trap_mret_commit_controller.v:62–84 commits at WB: writes mepc/mcause, clears MIE, and redirects to mtvec, or to mtvec + 4·cause in vectored mode.

F1 — Pulse interrupts are lost

irq_accept_E needs a valid, un-excepted, non-stalled instruction in EX and mstatus.MIE = 1 in the same cycle as the pulse. A pulse is dropped when any of these hold in that cycle:

  • MIE = 0: the core is inside any other ISR. This is the dominant case. A Timer0 tick that lands in a UART ISR is gone, so the RTOS tick drifts.
  • stallMUL: a multi-cycle divide is in EX (execute_cycle.v:440).
  • valid_E = 0: a bubble from a branch flush, load-use stall, IF/ID flush, or a long lsu_mem_stall on an AXI-Lite MMIO access.
  • An exception is already in EX (new_exc_valid_E or exc_valid_E).

The loss probability equals the fraction of cycles spent in those states, which rises with interrupt load. No existing test catches it. tb1_interrupts.sv holds each line high until the trap is taken, and the one single-cycle pulse (IRQ07, lines 736–740) is injected while MIE = 1 in straight-line code, so it is always accepted.

F2 / F3 — Priority order

The privileged spec fixes the order of the standard M-mode interrupts as MEI > MSI > MTI. top.v:253–254 checks MTIP before MSIP, and the SONIC-MCU summary repeats it as "MEIP > MTIP > MSIP". Spike and Sail follow the spec, so co-simulation diverges whenever MSIP and MTIP are pending together. Separately, all four FLIs outrank MTIP, which is backwards for a periodic tick. The spec leaves FLI order (causes ≥ 16) to the platform, so moving the timer above the FLIs is allowed.

F4 — mintprio is a cause threshold

Priority is welded to the cause ID. Applying the threshold after selection is correct only while priority order equals cause order. Any reordering, including the F2/F3 fix, makes a masked high-rank source hide an eligible lower-rank one. Nothing updates mintprio on trap entry or mret, so nesting is software-only and unsafe unless every ISR manages the threshold by hand.

F5 — How real is starvation?

On trap entry the hardware clears MIE, and priority is re-evaluated at every mret. A lower source starves only if a higher one is pending again at every mret. At 115,200 baud, UART delivers a byte every ~87 µs, which is ~8,700 cycles at 100 MHz. Even at 1 Mbaud it is ~1,000 cycles, against an ISR of ~100 cycles (est.). Legitimate traffic cannot starve anything. Starvation needs a fault: an ISR that does not clear its source, a chattering GPIO, or a missed I2C IACK.

Arbitration hardware should therefore bound the damage, not hide the bug. Round-robin among equal levels gives a provable bound. Weighted schemes add cost without changing that conclusion (section 4).

Area does not decide this. Every option except D is well under 1% of an RV32IM 5-stage core (est.). The deciding factors are verification risk, spec conformance (RISCOF and Sail must stay clean) and latency.

Policies considered and rejected

  • Weighted round-robin (per-peripheral weights): needs a credit counter per source (4–8 flops each) plus refill logic. Interrupts are events, not bandwidth streams, so weights give no better latency bound than levels plus RR, and they make the bound depend on runtime weight settings.
  • Aging (priority boost while waiting): latency becomes history-dependent, which is hard to specify and to prove formally. It also masks the stuck-source bugs that cause starvation.
  • Full CLIC now: the right long-term shape, but it changes trap entry (hardware vectoring loads a table entry through the data port) and needs CSRs that neither RISCOF nor mainline Spike checks. Too much risk this close to tape-out. Phase B keeps the same concepts so a later migration is mechanical (section 11).

5Is a PLIC needed for MCU scope?

No. A PLIC solves problems this SoC does not have:

  • It routes many sources to multiple harts and privilege contexts. There is one hart and M-mode only.
  • Five live sources fit in the 16 FLI slots, with 12 spare (mip[31:20]).
  • FLIs identify the source in mcause and vector directly. A PLIC funnels everything into cause 11, then the handler reads claim and writes complete over LSU → AXI-Lite → axi_lite_to_apb. That adds roughly 15–30 cycles per interrupt (est.).

What the PULP IP is, for the record: pulp-platform/rv_plic, forked from lowRISC, Apache-2.0, marked as an unstable development fork. plic_top takes parameters N_SOURCE, N_TARGET, MAX_PRIO, and reg_req_t/reg_rsp_t (the PULP register-bus interface). Ports are irq_sources_i, le_i (0 = level, 1 = edge per source) and eip_targets_o. It instantiates rv_plic_gateway, rv_plic_target per target and plic_regs. The rtl directory carries prim_subreg*, tlul_pkg.sv and top_pkg.sv, and the register map is generated by gen_plic_addrmap.py.

Revisit the PLIC when any of these becomes true:

  • More than 16 interrupt sources, beyond the FLI width.
  • A second hart, or S-mode for an OS.
  • A requirement to run unmodified Zephyr or Linux PLIC drivers.
  • Many slow sources, such as individual GPIO pins or an ADC, where 15–30 extra cycles do not matter.

The core already has the attachment point: ext_irq_i → mip[11]. Adding a PLIC later changes no core RTL.

6Proposed architecture

Gateway: irq_gateway.v (Phase A)

  • One bit per mip position. edge_sel = 1: a rising edge sets a sticky pend bit. edge_sel = 0: the input passes straight through (level).
  • The output is pend_q | rise, so an edge is visible in the same cycle it arrives. The gateway adds no latency.
  • Clear happens when the interrupt trap for that cause commits at WB, or when software writes 0 to that mip bit (csrc mip). Clearing at commit rather than at EX accept means that if an older instruction's exception flushes the interrupt packet, the interrupt stays pending and is retaken.
  • A new edge in the same cycle as a clear wins, so back-to-back events are not merged away.
  • Software cannot set MTIP or FLI bits: mip writes are clear-only for edge bits and ignored otherwise.
  • All sources are on clk_i, so no synchroniser is needed. Pad-level GPIO is already synchronised inside gpio.sv.

Reset compatibility

With all levels at 0, RR_EN = 0 and NEST_EN = 0, every pending source ties at effective level 1 and the fixed order decides. Phase B at reset therefore behaves exactly like Phase A, so every Phase A test runs unchanged on Phase B.

GPIO mode and edge setting must agree

If the BSP switches GPIO to glbl_intrpt_mode = 1 (sticky status, level output), it must also clear FLI_EDGE[0]. Otherwise a pin event that arrives while another status bit is still set produces no new rising edge and is missed.

Arbiter: irq_arbiter.v (Phase B1)

Starvation bound. With RR_EN = 1, a source that stays pending at the highest pending level is taken within N−1 grants, where N is the number of sources at that level. Property P4 proves this. A higher level can still starve a lower one, and that is intended: that is what a priority means. Choosing levels is the integrator's job, and section 10 gives a default.

Nesting (Phase B2)

  • On any trap entry: PREV_LVL ← CUR_LVL. On an interrupt trap, also CUR_LVL ← level taken.
  • On mret: CUR_LVL ← PREV_LVL.
  • The hardware keeps one level of history, so an ISR that re-enables MIE must save mintprio together with mepc and mstatus. That is the same rule the spec already imposes for mepc (section 10 has the wrapper).
  • Equal-level sources never preempt each other, because the take condition is strictly greater. This prevents same-level ping-pong.
  • With NEST_EN = 0, CUR_LVL is purely software-controlled. That is Phase B1 behaviour.

Regression and co-simulation

  • RISCOF rv32i_m I, M, Zicsr, privilege must stay at 100%. The new CSRs live in the custom range, which the arch tests do not touch. mip stays read-only-for-set, so its readback is unchanged.
  • Spike co-simulation: exclude CSRs 0x7C0–0x7C2 and mip[31:16] from state comparison, or add a Spike custom extension. The MEI > MSI > MTI fix removes the order divergence.
  • Functional coverage (SV covergroup): cause × level, RR grant sequences, nesting depth 0/1/2, and pulse arriving while MIE = 0 or during stallMUL.
  • Lint: the Verilator command from AGENTS.md plus SpyGlass on the three new or changed files.

10Bare-metal BSP

One header covers everything. The CSR numbers are stringified into the instruction, so plain GCC works without a custom toolchain.

ISRs that do not need preemption keep the existing plain __attribute__((interrupt("machine"))) form. crt0.S already writes mintprio = 0, which still means "accept all" under the new semantics.

11Future extensions

  • More sources: FLI slots 20–31 are free. Adding Timer1 compare as FLI[4] (cause 20) is SoC wiring only. Set its level, edge bit and mie bit in software; the core RTL is untouched.
  • PLIC on MEIP (Phase C, figure 7) when a section 5 trigger is met. Latency-critical sources stay on direct FLIs and slow ones fan in through the PLIC.
  • CLIC migration: the Phase B fields map one-to-one onto CLIC concepts. mintlvl corresponds to the level bits of clicintctl, CUR_LVL to mintthresh/mintstatus.mil, PREV_LVL to mcause.mpil, and FLI_EDGE to clicintattr.trig. Keeping the irq.h API stable means drivers do not change. The PULP CLIC is already in rtl/ips/clic-master/.
  • Trim for area: a NUM_FLI parameter on the gateway and arbiter (default 16) lets the tape-out build implement only the sources that exist.
