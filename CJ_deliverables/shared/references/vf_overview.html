1. Purpose and how to read the plan

The objective of the plan is not "find bugs". It is to be able to make a defensible statement that the SoC meets its specification and to show the evidence for that statement (Rev B 2). Three consequences shape everything else:

  • Every requirement needs a check and every check needs a test. The chain is generated into a traceability matrix, not maintained by hand.
  • An absence of findings means something only if the mechanism could have produced one. This is the organising principle (Rev B 1.6): a check that cannot fail is not a check. Each checker therefore carries a proof of viability: a fault-injection case, an assertion self-test or a directed negative test.
  • A run that exercised nothing is a failure, not a pass. This is why gates exist in tiers.
Evidence rule used across these pages. A verification result is "PASS for a stated boundary" (a named RTL identity, configuration and check set). It is never expressed as a percentage of requirements. At the time of review P0 (bring-up) is in progress, not exited: 13 of 20 exit items met, 5 partial, 2 not met (Verification/status/PHASE_STATUS.md).

2. The whole framework on one page

Figure 1 is the master picture. The design under test sits in the centre. Stimulus (five sources, top) drives it. The seven layers (left) say where in the design a check applies. Observers, checkers and models (right) judge what the design did. Coverage and the reporter (below) turn that into evidence, gates decide pass, fail or untested, and phases order the build-up. The environment box under the DUT is the generated, hashed run identity that makes any result citable. Everything in this picture is what Rev B specifies; the figure is not a claim that it is built. SPECIFIED

Two properties of the picture are worth stating in words, because they are the point of the design of the plan (Rev B 2):

  • Each band can fail a run alone; no band can detect everything alone. One band reporting one finding fails the run and cannot be outvoted. For every defect class at least one band cannot see it, so the sign-off claim is the conjunction of all four bands, and each band must separately be able to report a finding.
  • Gates are tiered by what evidence they need. Rev A demanded all seven gates of every run, which is impossible because G2, G4 and G6 are decidable only from artefacts that accumulate across commits. A gate that cannot be evaluated is reported as untested, never as passed, and never as a waiver.

Details: gates in vf_regression_gates.html, checkers in vf_checking_scoreboards.html, coverage in vf_coverage.html, phases in vf_phases_overview.html, layers and environment in vf_strategy_env.html.

5.2 Three facts Rev A got wrong

  • There is no always-on watchdog in this SoC. aon_timer is real RTL but instantiated nowhere and absent from soc_subsystem.f. Its host port is TL-UL, not APB, it has its own clock and reset, and 14 registers. EXISTS as a file, not as an integrated block.
  • soft_rst_ni is an external input. Nothing inside the SoC drives it; warm boot is an externally requested event, not a watchdog consequence.
  • mip[20] is Timer 0 channel 0 overflow, not a watchdog. The SoC wires it but the core discards it, so mip[20] to mip[26] are provably zero in the RTL as it stands. Existing firmware must not depend on those bits.

6. Status vocabulary

Every statement in Rev B carries one of four build states, and this documentation set follows the same discipline with a fifth, OPEN, for facts that are unknown or disputed (Rev B 1.2).

TagMeaning in Rev BWhat a reader may conclude
EXISTSPresent in the repository today, with a locator; falsifiable by reading the fileThe statement can be checked against the file now
SPECIFIEDRequired by the plan, absent today, with an itemised RTL-change countIt is a requirement, not a result. Nothing here proves it works
ASSUMED(D-nnn)A directed default adopted because the RTL is silent, with reasoning recordedA decision, not a measurement. A REFUTED decision may not be relied on
TBD(D-nnn)Required and not established; the tag names the decision that will supply a defaultDo not substitute an untagged number
OPENUnknown or disputed (this documentation set's label)Treat as unresolved
Two cautions. First, Rev B was written before most verification was built, so many "specified" statements are plans, not outcomes: its quality goals are targets, not current state. Second, the privileged ISA version is TBD (no version string exists in the repository), which Rev B calls the plan's largest specification gap (Rev B 1.4).
