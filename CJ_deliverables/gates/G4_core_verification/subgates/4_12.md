# CJ subgate 4.12: Interrupt entry (CJ scope)

**Official workbook status:** In Progress (sheet G4, supplied workbook).

**CJ scope:** interrupt entry only. Reset verification is excluded from this deliverable.

**Engineering progress: PARTIALLY COMPLETE.** The scoped timer-entry check is done: cause 7, committed PC boundary, handler/MRET and replay are checked in the retained campaign. The IRQ unit campaign also passes. This does not establish all-source/all-condition pipeline entry or complete entry-to-commit coverage.

**Remaining scope:** independent acceptance timing, full trap-state/packet checks and precise effects under stalls/ordering remain unclosed. The checklist names no separate entry-to-commit gate. G6.5 covers the end-to-end interrupt path and G6.13 covers trap robustness under load; these are later cross-references, not evidence that every commit check has been reassigned. ## Testbench, evidence and remaining work

There is a current core-level interrupt-entry check in `minstret_retirement/checklist/tb_checklist.sv`: it pulses the timer IRQ, checks committed interrupt cause 7 and the precise committed PC window, inserts the assembled handler into the expected retirement path, and requires the interrupted instruction to replay after MRET. The retained 11-Nov replay reports 12/12 runs passed and 29 bins closed across its scoped campaign, including waits 0/1/4/9. This is useful evidence for the tested timer-entry path, not complete interrupt-entry closure: the README notes that acceptance-cycle prediction is not independent, and nested/multiple sources and every MRET/backpressure interaction remain open. Entry acceptance is a distinct claim from retirement counting: the campaign counts retired instructions and checks the tested timer path; it does not establish that other sources, mask/threshold boundaries or stalled pipelines accept correctly.
