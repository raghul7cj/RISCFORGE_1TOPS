Regression Model and Gate Policy

How Rev B turns a pile of test runs into a verdict: the regression flow, the seven canonical gates G0 to G6, the three tiers they are decided at, the hard line between checker viability (G2) and coverage (G4), and the rule that stops any phase from demanding a gate that a later phase produces.

1. What a regression is, and the two verbs

Rev B defines a regression as three things together: a manifest (what runs), a schedule (when) and a gate policy (what the results must satisfy). The gate policy matters most, because it separates a regression that measures the design from one that only reports on the harness. SPECIFIED

The gate vocabulary is stated once, in Table 6.1 of Rev B, and every other section refers to it by identifier. If a figure and the register disagree, the register wins and the figure is the defect. This page follows the same rule: it summarises the register and never invents a different wording.

Rev B uses exactly two verbs for checks, because in SVA a property that "fires" has failed:

  • reported: a checker or assertion produced a unique-ID finding.
  • evaluated: the check was sampled with its enabling (antecedent) condition satisfied.

A gate that cannot be evaluated is untested. That is a verdict of its own: never a pass, never a waiver.

4. Three tiers and what feeds what

Rev A required all seven gates of every run. Rev B's correction is that the gates consume different evidence:

  • Per-run (G0, G1, G3): decidable from one run's own artefacts, with no cross-run state.
  • Per-candidate (G2, G4, G6): decidable only from the union of a candidate's runs plus campaign artefacts that accumulate (viability ledger, merged coverage database, generated matrix). Requiring them per commit is the Rev A defect recorded as finding 15.
  • Release (G5): evaluated once per release, from a full compliance sweep and the formal property register.

Only the release tier blocks sign-off, but the release gate in turn requires the others (see sign-off). Figure 2 shows which tier feeds which.

5. G2 versus G4: viability versus coverage

Rev A used one word, "coverage", for two obligations, and G2 and G4 overlapped. Rev B separates them with three terms over one unit, the check ID (Table 6.2):

TermPredicate actually measuredRecorded by
evaluatedantecedent_satisfied_count(check_id) >= 1: the DUT satisfied the antecedent at least once in the campaignCoverage database
non-vacuousRecorded evidence the check can report: a deliberately injected fault made it report its unique-ID finding (negative_case_pass), or a formal result shows the antecedent is satisfiable (vacuity_proved)Viability ledger
vacuousEvaluated and has no viability record: the check was sampled and still cannot report(derived: coverage database minus ledger)

The boundary is hard and neither gate substitutes for the other. G2 asks whether the checker can report; its evidence is a fault-injection or self-test artefact. G4 asks whether the design behaviour was observed; its evidence is a bin count. G2 is stated over check IDs and G4 over coverage bins, and a bin that corresponds to a check ID contributes to G4 only; it never discharges G2. Figure 3 shows the four combinations.

Rev A required phase P4 to satisfy G5, while formal closed in P5, an unsatisfiable phase graph. D-023 fixes it with these edges: SPECIFIED

Consequences: P4 does not require G5; P4 produces the compliance artefact and P5 the proof log, and G5 is stamped once, in P6 (Release). Rev B 6 states that no per-candidate gate depends on formal. Rev B 21.1 additionally separates this from a phase-entry condition (such as core trust CT-1 to CT-5), which blocks a phase from starting but is not a gate and is never stamped. 8. The verdict record

Every gate verdict is decided from a small set of artefacts. What a run must leave behind is defined across Rev B 5, 6, 19 and 21.7: SPECIFIED

  • Run identity (Table 5.2, eight fields): RTL SHA-256, filelist SHA-256, tool version, seed, plusargs, scenario identifier, verdict, artefact locator. Section 21.7 adds ID-1, ID-2 and ID-3 and the identity hashes to every artefact header; a log missing any field is not evidence.
  • Counter block (G1 operands): instructions_retired, axi_transfers, apb_transfers, attestation_words_written.
  • Checker operands (G3): open_findings, unexplained_ring_drops, scoreboard_drain_satisfied.
  • Verdict per gate: pass, fail, or untested, never a blank and never a waiver.

Per-candidate gates read the viability ledger, the merged coverage database with the waiver ledger and exclusion register, and the generated traceability matrix. No operand is a duration in seconds or a count of bus beats; time is counted in clk_i cycles (ASSUMED, D-036).
